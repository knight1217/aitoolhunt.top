AI Fashion Design & Virtual Try-On Tools 2026

AI is transforming fashion — from generating original clothing designs to letting shoppers try on clothes virtually. Here are the tools reshaping the industry.

Fashion Meets Artificial Intelligence

Fashion has always been about creativity — but in 2026, AI is augmenting that creativity at every stage. Designers use AI to generate hundreds of variations from a single sketch. Retailers use virtual try-on to reduce returns by 25-40%. And trend forecasters use AI to predict what colors and silhouettes will dominate next season. Here are the best tools in each category.

AI Clothing Design & Generation

The New Black — AI Fashion Design Platform

Price: Free tier. Pro from $15/month.

The New Black is purpose-built for fashion designers. Describe a garment — "oversized beige trench coat with exaggerated lapels, streetwear aesthetic" — and the AI generates photorealistic designs on model silhouettes. You can iterate by specifying fabrics, colors, and details. The platform also includes a community gallery where designers share and remix AI-generated concepts.

Standout feature: "Moodboard to Collection" — upload inspiration images and the AI generates a cohesive 10-piece collection.

CALA — AI-Powered Fashion Business

Price: From $10/month.

CALA is an end-to-end platform for fashion brands — AI design generation, tech pack creation, sourcing, and production management. Describe your design and CALA generates detailed tech packs (the technical specifications manufacturers need), complete with material suggestions and measurement tables. It connects directly to a network of vetted manufacturers.

Best for: Independent fashion brands that want to go from AI concept to physical product without hiring a full design team.

Midjourney — For Conceptual Fashion Design

Price: From $10/month.

While not fashion-specific, Midjourney has become a staple in fashion design workflows. Designers use it for concept exploration, textile pattern generation, and editorial-quality lookbook imagery. The latest V7 model handles fabric draping, textures, and lighting with remarkable realism. Many professional designers start their creative process here before moving to specialized tools.

AI Virtual Try-On

Vue.ai — AI Styling & Virtual Try-On for Retail

Price: Custom (enterprise).

Vue.ai provides AI virtual try-on for major fashion retailers. Shoppers upload a photo or enter measurements, and the AI shows how garments will look on their actual body type — not just on a generic model. The technology also powers "Complete the Look" recommendations, increasing average order value by 15-20%. Used by brands like Diesel, Nordstrom, and ThredUp.

Google's Virtual Try-On (VTO) — Shopping Feature

Price: Free (integrated into Google Shopping).

Google Shopping now includes AI virtual try-on for apparel across hundreds of brands. The AI generates realistic images of garments on models with diverse body types (sizes XXS-4XL), skin tones, and poses. Built on diffusion models trained specifically for garment visualization. Available directly in search results — no separate app needed.

AI Trend Prediction

Heuritech — AI Fashion Forecasting

Price: Custom (enterprise, typically $10K+/year).

Heuritech analyzes millions of social media images daily to detect emerging fashion trends 6-12 months before they hit mainstream. Its AI can quantify the rise of a specific silhouette, color, or pattern across regions and demographics. Used by Louis Vuitton, Dior, and Adidas to inform design decisions and inventory planning.

The Impact on Fashion Retail

Virtual try-on alone is transforming online fashion retail. Return rates for online clothing purchases average 30-40% — and 70% of those returns are due to fit issues. AI virtual try-on reduces returns by an estimated 25-40%, saving retailers billions in reverse logistics costs. For independent designers, AI tools have democratized the entire process — a single person can now design, produce, and sell a clothing line without a fashion degree or massive upfront investment.
